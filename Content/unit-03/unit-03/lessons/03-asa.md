## concept

The **Angle-Side-Angle (ASA) Congruence Postulate** states: *If two angles and the included side of one triangle are congruent to two angles and the included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.* As with SAS, the word "included" is essential — the side must be the one that *connects* the two given angle vertices, sitting directly between them.

To identify the included side, locate the two angles and find the segment that has one angle vertex at each of its endpoints. In △ABC, if the two given angles are ∠A and ∠C, the included side is AC — the segment that runs from vertex A directly to vertex C. The angle at vertex B is the third angle, not part of the ASA setup. If the given angles were ∠A and ∠B instead, the included side would be AB.

Why does ASA guarantee congruence? Two angles of a triangle determine its shape — the third angle is forced by the Triangle Angle Sum Theorem (∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°). Once shape is fixed, specifying any one side determines the triangle's *scale*. The included side provides that scale anchor. Think of it this way: a triangle's angles give it its "look," and fixing one side's length tells you how big it is. Together, they fully determine every other measurement.

An important consequence: if two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another, then the third angles *must* also be congruent (since the sum is always 180°). This means ASA and AAS (the next theorem) are closely related — the difference lies only in whether the known side is the included one or a non-included one. For ASA, the side must be enclosed between the two known angles.

ASA arises naturally with parallel lines: when a transversal crosses two parallel lines, it creates pairs of congruent alternate interior angles. If two triangles share the transversal as a side, those alternate interior angles (one at each endpoint of the transversal) give ASA directly. This connection to Unit 2's parallel lines material is a recurring payoff of building geometry systematically.

## reflection

ASA reveals a deep symmetry in geometry: you can describe a triangle either by its sides (SSS) or by angles plus a single anchoring measurement (ASA). This duality — shape information versus size information — runs throughout mathematics. In trigonometry, it corresponds to the Law of Sines, which lets you solve a triangle when you know two angles and the enclosed side.

ASA connects most naturally to the study of parallel lines from Unit 2. Alternate interior angles, co-interior angles, and corresponding angles — all the results you proved about parallel lines — become tools for establishing the angle pairs needed for ASA. A triangle proof that begins "since lines are parallel, alternate interior angles are congruent" is building directly on your earlier work.

Next comes AAS, which relaxes the "included" requirement for the side. Understanding why AAS is valid — and why ASA and AAS both work while SSA does not — is a key goal of this unit. As you master each postulate, you are building a toolkit for the two-column proofs in Lesson 7, where you will chain these results together to prove properties of quadrilaterals, circles, and other figures.

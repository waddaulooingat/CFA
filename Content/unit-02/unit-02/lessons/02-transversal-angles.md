# Skill 2 — Angles Formed by a Transversal

**Skill ID:** `geo-u2-transversal-angles`
**Difficulty band:** 1
**Prereqs:** geo-u2-parallel-basics, geo-u1-angle-measure

---

## Concept

A **transversal** is a line that crosses two or more other lines. When a transversal cuts two lines, **eight angles** form — four at each intersection. We name them ∠1 through ∠8, usually numbered around the page from top-left to bottom-right.

Those eight angles fall into a small number of **named pairs** based on their position. This skill is pure vocabulary: you need to be able to look at a diagram and name every pair. The next skill (Parallel Line Theorems) is where these pairs become *useful*.

**The four interior angles** sit between the two crossed lines. The four exterior angles sit outside.

**Six pair types you'll need by name:**

- **Corresponding angles** — same position at each intersection (e.g., both upper-right). One angle from the top intersection, one from the bottom. Four corresponding pairs.
- **Alternate interior angles (AIA)** — interior, on opposite sides of the transversal. Two AIA pairs.
- **Alternate exterior angles (AEA)** — exterior, on opposite sides of the transversal. Two AEA pairs.
- **Co-interior angles** (also called *same-side interior* or *consecutive interior*) — interior, on the *same* side of the transversal. Two co-interior pairs.
- **Vertical angles** — opposite angles at a single intersection. Two vertical pairs at each intersection (four total).
- **Linear pair** — adjacent angles on a single straight line. Many of these.

**The picture you need to memorize.** Two horizontal-ish parallel lines cut by a slanted transversal. Number the angles at the top intersection 1-2-3-4 going clockwise from upper-left. Number the angles at the bottom intersection 5-6-7-8 the same way.

Then:
- Corresponding: (1,5), (2,6), (3,7), (4,8)
- Alternate interior: (3,5), (4,6)
- Alternate exterior: (1,7), (2,8)
- Co-interior: (4,5), (3,6)
- Vertical at top: (1,3), (2,4)
- Vertical at bottom: (5,7), (6,8)

A useful trick: **corresponding** angles are the easiest to spot — they're always in the *same position* relative to their intersection. Find the upper-left at each crossing; those two are corresponding. Once you can spot a corresponding pair, the others follow.

A prompt will appear ("Click an alternate interior pair") and you click two angles. Correct clicks light up green; incorrect light up red. The drill cycles through all six pair types. Aim for getting each on the first try.

*Why this matters:* in three lessons, you'll be using these names without thinking. Right now you should drill the naming until it's instant.

## Worked Example

> Two lines are cut by a transversal forming eight angles ∠1–∠8. Identify each named pair below.
> (Using the standard numbering: top intersection ∠1–∠4 clockwise from upper-left; bottom intersection ∠5–∠8 the same way.)

**Pair (a): ∠2 and ∠6.** Both are in the *upper-right* position at their intersections — ∠2 at the top crossing, ∠6 at the bottom. Same position = **corresponding angles**.

**Pair (b): ∠3 and ∠5.** ∠3 sits in the interior, lower-right at the top intersection. ∠5 sits in the interior, upper-left at the bottom intersection. Both interior, opposite sides of the transversal = **alternate interior angles**.

**Pair (c): ∠4 and ∠5.** ∠4 interior at the top intersection, lower-left. ∠5 interior at the bottom intersection, upper-left. Both interior, same side of the transversal = **co-interior angles** (also called same-side interior).

**Pair (d): ∠1 and ∠3.** Both at the *top* intersection, on opposite sides of the crossing point. **Vertical angles.**

**Pair (e): ∠1 and ∠7.** Both *exterior* (outside the two lines), on opposite sides of the transversal. **Alternate exterior angles.**

## Reflection

A transversal makes eight angles. Six pair types: corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, co-interior, vertical, linear pair. Memorize the layout — corresponding-as-same-position is your anchor.

Note: in this lesson, **none** of these pairs have any guaranteed angle relationship. We're just naming. The next skill is where the relationships kick in — but ONLY if the two crossed lines are parallel.
